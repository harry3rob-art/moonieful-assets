Strong work can be hard to explain

  • A student explains a business idea for the fifth time, and the listener still asks, "So what do you actually do?"
  • A new owner hears from people who were never going to buy, while the right customers scroll past.
  • A website looks finished, yet it doesn't bring in the people it was built for.

Sound familiar? The work behind each of these is often good. What's missing is a clear way to say it, and Moonieful has a name for that: Brand Fog.

What Brand Fog is

Brand Fog is the distance between how good someone's work is and what a stranger understands about it. Students find what's unclear first, before they touch the logo, the website or the pitch. Once they can see the fog in their own words, the fix is often a few clear sentences a customer can repeat.

What would your students do in each session?

Here's a closer look at each format, with a proposed syllabus your program can adjust to fit its schedule and its students.

Introduction

Brand Fog Workshop

60 minutes

A first session for a whole class or cohort, with no preparation needed. Participants learn what Brand Fog is, find the one place in their own business idea where a stranger gets lost, and practice a one-sentence introduction until a classmate can say it back. The workshop works on its own or as the opening session for either of the deeper formats.

By the end, students can:

  • Name the fog zone where a stranger is most likely to get lost in their business.
  • Write a one-sentence introduction that says who they serve, what they solve and why it matters.
  • Test that sentence on a classmate and revise it until the classmate can say it back.
See the proposed syllabus
  1. 10 minutes
    The “so what do you do?” test

    Each participant describes their business idea to a partner in 30 seconds, and the partner says back what they heard. The difference between the two versions is where the session starts.

  2. 10 minutes
    What Brand Fog is

    Why good work still gets misunderstood, and why the message comes before the logo, the website or the pitch.

  3. 15 minutes
    The five fog zones

    The offer, the audience, the change a customer gets, the story and the trust signals. Participants run a quick self-check on each zone using their own idea.

  4. 10 minutes
    Find your fog

    Each participant picks the one zone where customers are most likely to get lost first.

  5. 15 minutes
    Your one-sentence introduction

    Participants write one sentence that says who they serve, what they solve and why it matters, then test it on a new partner and tighten it.

Guided application

Brand Clarity Practicum

90 to 120 minutes

A hands-on working session for participants who already have something written, like a pitch, a bio, a social profile or a website. They work on their own words with feedback from Miki and their classmates, and they keep rewriting until a stranger can say back what they offer. The 120-minute version adds a second round of rewriting and feedback.

By the end, students can:

  • Describe their customer in the plain words a customer would use.
  • Rewrite one real piece of their own messaging, starting with the problem their customer feels.
  • Give and receive feedback using the four-part feedback rubric.
See the proposed syllabus
  1. Before
    Bring your own words

    Each participant brings one piece of their own messaging: a pitch, a bio, a social profile or a website.

  2. 10 minutes
    The stranger read

    Partners swap materials and write one line saying what they think the other business does, and the two answers show where the fog is.

  3. 20 minutes
    Build the core message

    Who you serve, what you solve and why it matters, written in plain words a customer would use.

  4. 15 minutes
    Find the words customers use

    Participants gather phrases from customers, classmates and real conversations into a messaging bank, then swap industry jargon for those words.

  5. 20 minutes
    Rewrite your piece

    Each participant rewrites the material they brought, starting with the problem their customer feels, then what changes, how the offer works, proof and the next step.

  6. 20 minutes
    Feedback round

    Small groups and Miki give feedback on each rewrite. Participants with a website can scan it with SnapGist to see how a first-time visitor reads it.

  7. 5 minutes
    One change to make this week

    Each participant picks the change they'll make first and when they'll make it.

Deeper learning

Pilot Learning Intensive

Six weeks

A proposed six-week series with one session a week and a short task between sessions, so participants apply Brand Fog to their own business while the program is still running. Each week builds on the last: participants find their fog, build their core message, test it with real people and finish by sharing a before-and-after version of their message. Session length and group size are set with your program, and each week pairs with chapters of the Brand Fog book for anyone who wants to read along.

By the end, students can:

  • Test their core message with at least five people outside class and record what each person says back.
  • Build a messaging bank of at least 10 phrases from real customers and conversations.
  • Draft a page or pitch that includes all seven Clarity Page parts.
  • Present a before-and-after message and a 30-day plan for using it.
See the proposed syllabus
  1. Week one
    See the fog

    What Brand Fog is, the five fog zones and the stranger read. Between sessions, participants ask three people what they think their business does and write down the exact words. Reading: chapters one and three.

  2. Week two
    Message before design

    Why a new logo or website can't fix an unclear message, and how to listen to what customers already say. Between sessions, participants start a messaging bank from real questions, comments and reviews. Reading: chapters two and four.

  3. Week three
    The core message

    Who you serve, what you solve and why it matters. Between sessions, participants test their core message on five people outside the class and note what each person says back. Reading: chapter five.

  4. Week four
    Words customers use

    Voice traits, the messaging bank and trading jargon for plain words. Between sessions, participants rewrite their bio or pitch using their messaging bank. Reading: chapter six.

  5. Week five
    A page or pitch that makes saying yes easy

    The seven-part Clarity Page: the problem, what's really happening, what changes, how the offer works, proof, why you and the next step. Between sessions, participants draft their page or pitch, and those with a website scan it with SnapGist. Reading: chapter seven.

  6. Week six
    Share and plan

    Participants present their before-and-after message and get feedback from the group and Miki. Each one leaves with a 30-day plan for using their message in content and conversations. Reading: chapters eight and nine.

What does a class with Miki look like?

Every session starts with your students' own business ideas. Students try their message on a classmate, hear what came across, rewrite it and try again, so by the end of a session they've practiced the skill themselves. Each format ends with one change a student can make that week, because a message only gets clearer once a student starts using it.

Sample lesson for one class period: Who is your customer?

A 75-minute lesson that fits inside an existing entrepreneurship or business course.

  1. 10 minutes
    Warm-up

    Each student writes one sentence about who their business is for and reads it to a partner.

  2. 15 minutes
    Mini-lesson

    Why a business that's for everyone reaches no one, with before-and-after examples from the Brand Fog book.

  3. 20 minutes
    Customer profile

    Students describe one real person who'd buy: that person's situation, the problem they'd say out loud and the words they'd use.

  4. 20 minutes
    Rewrite and peer review

    Students rewrite their opening sentence for that person, then score a partner's sentence with the feedback rubric.

  5. 10 minutes
    Exit ticket

    Each student turns in the revised sentence and one change their partner's feedback led to.

How students give each other feedback

Students score each other's message on four questions, marking each one Not yet, Partly or Yes.

  • Clear customer

    Can a stranger tell who this business is for?

  • Clear problem

    Can a stranger name the problem the business solves?

  • Customer's words

    Does the message use words a customer would say, with no industry jargon?

  • Clear next step

    Does a stranger know what to do next?
