The real problem

You've already tried the obvious fixes.

You rewrote your homepage. Maybe more than once. You updated your photos, tightened your offer page, started posting more consistently.

The right clients are still finding your page and leaving. And the fixes didn't fail because you did them wrong. Redesigns, templates, and AI copy are what the market sells because they're easy to sell, not because they find what's actually in the way.

Here's the tell. You can explain what you do in person, and the person across the table gets it every time. Your page can't give a stranger that same in-person explanation yet. Somewhere between how good you are and what a stranger reads in that first scroll, your value is getting lost in translation, and every lost translation is a client who picked someone else.

Brand Fog

The haze a stranger sees before they ever meet you.

Your business is real and your results are real. Your page is what a stranger meets first, and it has a few seconds to do the explaining for you.

Brand Fog lives in three places. Every visitor reads all three in the first scroll, whether they know they're doing it or not.

  • 1 Message

    What you say you do and who you say you help. When your message is foggy, a visitor can't tell if you're speaking to them, and a visitor who can't tell assumes you're not.

  • 2 Visuals

    How your brand looks and feels. When visuals are mismatched or generic, your work behind them looks less valuable than it is.

  • 3 Positioning

    How clearly you stand apart from everyone else in your category. When your positioning is foggy, a visitor has no reason to choose you over someone cheaper.

The Brand Fog Book shows you how to read all three signals from the outside, the way a stranger reads them, so you can see your own brand clearly for the first time.

Why it's invisible from the inside

You're the one person who can't read your own page.

When you look at your own homepage, your brain quietly fills in everything the words leave out. You know what you meant. You know the client you were picturing when you wrote it. And you know the results sitting behind that one vague line. A stranger has none of that, so they read only what's actually on the screen.

That's why looking at your own page with fresh eyes never works. You can't un-know your own business. What you can do is borrow a procedure for reading it the way someone who's never met you reads it, one signal at a time, and that's what the 88 pages hand you.
